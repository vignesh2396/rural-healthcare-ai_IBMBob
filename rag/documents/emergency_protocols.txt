EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS & FIRST RESPONSE NAVIGATION GUIDE
=======================================================
Source: National Health Mission / Emergency Response Services India
Document Type: Emergency Navigation Protocols (Not for Clinical Use)

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER: These are navigation and first-response GUIDANCE protocols only.
They do NOT replace professional emergency medical care. Always call 108 immediately.

=======================================================
SECTION 1: CARDIAC EMERGENCY NAVIGATION
=======================================================

Signs that may indicate a cardiac emergency (Navigation Alert Keywords):
- Chest pain, pressure, tightness, or heaviness
- Pain spreading to arm, jaw, neck, or back
- Difficulty breathing with chest discomfort
- Cold sweating, nausea, dizziness
- Feeling of doom

IMMEDIATE NAVIGATION ACTION:
1. Call 108 (ambulance) IMMEDIATELY — do not wait
2. Keep the person calm and still — do not let them walk around
3. Loosen tight clothing (collar, belt)
4. Ask if they have any prescribed cardiac medication (nitroglycerin — patient should administer if prescribed)
5. Do NOT give food, water, or any medication you are not sure about
6. Stay with the person and stay on phone with 108

Nearest Facility Type Required: Hospital with Cardiac ICU / Cath Lab
Government Option: District Hospital (24x7 emergency)

=======================================================
SECTION 2: STROKE NAVIGATION
=======================================================

FAST Recognition (Navigation Alert Keywords):
- Face drooping (one side of face drooping)
- Arm weakness (one arm cannot be raised)
- Speech difficulty (slurred, confused, unable to speak)
- Time — call 108 immediately

CRITICAL: Stroke treatment window is 3-4.5 hours (thrombolysis)
Every minute of delay causes more brain cell loss

IMMEDIATE NAVIGATION ACTION:
1. Call 108 IMMEDIATELY — note the time symptoms started
2. Do NOT give food, water, or aspirin
3. Keep person calm, do not let them sleep if conscious
4. Note time of symptom onset for doctors
5. Go to hospital with CT scan and neurology department

Nearest Facility Required: District Hospital or above with CT scan

=======================================================
SECTION 3: TRAUMA & ACCIDENT NAVIGATION
=======================================================

NAVIGATION ACTION for Road Accident / Fall / Major Injury:
1. Call 108 immediately
2. Do NOT move the person if spinal injury is suspected (neck or back injury)
3. If bleeding: apply pressure with clean cloth — do not remove cloth
4. Keep person warm
5. Do not give food or water if surgery may be needed
6. Note type of injury for 108 dispatcher

Nearest Facility Required: Hospital with trauma/emergency surgery, blood bank
Government Option: District Hospital Trauma Centre

=======================================================
SECTION 4: OBSTETRIC EMERGENCY NAVIGATION
=======================================================

Signs requiring IMMEDIATE navigation:
- Heavy vaginal bleeding during pregnancy
- Severe abdominal pain during pregnancy
- Convulsions during pregnancy (eclampsia signs)
- No fetal movement for 12+ hours (last trimester)
- Premature labor (before 37 weeks)
- Fever + foul-smelling discharge after delivery
- Excessive bleeding after delivery

IMMEDIATE ACTION:
1. Call 108 (102 — National Ambulance Service, free for pregnant women)
2. Keep mother calm and lying down (left lateral position preferred)
3. Do NOT give food, water, or medicines without medical guidance
4. Go to hospital with 24x7 obstetric services

Nearest Facility Required: CHC with SNCU/NBSU or District Hospital with NICU

=======================================================
SECTION 5: PEDIATRIC EMERGENCY NAVIGATION
=======================================================

Signs in children requiring URGENT navigation:
- High fever (>104°F / 40°C) with stiffness, rash, confusion
- Difficulty breathing, fast breathing
- Seizure or convulsion
- Persistent vomiting and diarrhea with dehydration signs
- Loss of consciousness
- Severe allergic reaction (swelling of face/throat)

IMMEDIATE ACTION:
1. Call 108 for any pediatric emergency
2. For fever: Use paracetamol only (age-appropriate dose) while seeking care
3. For seizure: Place child on side, do not hold limbs down, time the seizure
4. For dehydration: ORS (Oral Rehydration Salts) if child can swallow — available free at PHC

Nearest Facility: CHC with SNCU or District Hospital Pediatric ward

=======================================================
SECTION 6: SNAKE BITE & POISONING NAVIGATION
=======================================================

Snake Bite NAVIGATION:
1. Call 108 immediately
2. Keep bitten limb still and below heart level
3. Remove jewelry/tight clothing near bite
4. Do NOT cut, suck, or tie tourniquet
5. Mark the edge of swelling and note time
6. Go to hospital — Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) available at CHC/District Hospital (free)

Poisoning/Chemical Ingestion:
1. Call 108 immediately
2. Note the name of substance ingested, time, and amount
3. Do NOT induce vomiting unless instructed by medical professional
4. Bring the container/substance to hospital

National Poison Control: AIIMS Delhi: 011-26593677 / 1800-11-6117

=======================================================
SECTION 7: MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS NAVIGATION
=======================================================

Navigation for Suicidal Ideation / Mental Health Crisis:
1. Stay with the person — do not leave them alone
2. Listen without judgment
3. Remove access to potential means (sharp objects, medications)
4. Call crisis helplines:
   - iCall: 9152987821 (Mon-Sat, 8am-10pm)
   - Vandrevala Foundation: 1860-2662-345 (24x7)
   - KIRAN: 1800-599-0019 (toll-free, 24x7)
   - NIMHANS: 080-46110007
5. If immediate physical danger: Call 108 or 100

Nearest Facility: District Hospital Psychiatry OPD or Government Mental Health Centre

=======================================================
SECTION 8: ANAPHYLAXIS (SEVERE ALLERGIC REACTION) NAVIGATION
=======================================================

Signs (Navigation Alert Keywords):
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing after eating/medication/sting
- Throat swelling, difficulty swallowing
- Rapid heartbeat, dizziness
- Severe skin rash, hives all over body
- Loss of consciousness after allergen exposure

IMMEDIATE ACTION:
1. Call 108 IMMEDIATELY — anaphylaxis is life-threatening
2. If Epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) is available and prescribed: use it
3. Lay person flat with legs raised (unless breathing difficulty — then sit up)
4. Do NOT give oral medication if throat is swelling
5. Go to hospital with emergency services

=======================================================
SECTION 9: GENERAL EMERGENCY NAVIGATION CHECKLIST
=======================================================

When calling 108, provide:
□ Your exact location (village, landmark, nearest main road)
□ Type of emergency (accident, chest pain, pregnancy, etc.)
□ Number of people affected
□ Your callback phone number
□ Nearest hospital you know of

Important Numbers to Save:
- 108: Emergency ambulance
- 102: Free ambulance (pregnant women & children)
- 104: Health helpline (advice, teleconsultation)
- 1800-11-6117: Poison control (toll-free)
- 1800-599-0019: Mental health (toll-free)
- Local PHC number
- Local hospital number
